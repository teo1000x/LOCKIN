## 2. What "Good" Looks Like Operationally

A competent agency or in-house marketer running a local-service account performs roughly this cadence:

### Weekly (30–45 min/account, same day each week is the industry norm)
- **Search term / query report review**: scan for irrelevant queries, add negative keywords. This is described as needing to happen weekly or spend leaks accumulate.
- **Budget pacing check**: verify month-to-date spend against target (e.g., by day 12 of a 30-day month, ~40% of budget should be spent, with ~10% variance tolerance).
- **Bid/bid-strategy adjustments**: manual bid tweaks, dayparting if data supports it (e.g., pause overnight for non-emergency trades).
- **Lead quality spot-check**: listen to a sample of recorded calls or review form-fill notes to catch spam/junk leads and feed exclusions back into targeting.
- **Ad schedule / geo-radius sanity check**, especially after any service-area change.

### Monthly
- **Creative/ad copy testing**: rotate headlines, test offers (e.g., "$50 off tune-up" vs. "Free estimate").
- **Landing page conversion review**: bounce rate, form completion rate, call-button click rate.
- **Seasonality adjustments**: e.g., HVAC contractors ramp budget 50–100% during heat waves/cold snaps and pause or shift emphasis on maintenance messaging in shoulder seasons; roofers pivot budget hard into storm season.
- **Channel mix rebalancing**: shifting budget between Google Search, LSA, and Meta based on cost-per-booked-job, not just cost-per-lead.
- **Full account audit**: keyword expansion, audience refresh, competitor gap analysis.

### Quarterly / Ad hoc
- **Offer testing**: new promotions, seasonal specials, financing offers (common in HVAC/dental/med spa).
- **Geo-targeting radius recalibration**: matching actual service area (many agencies find accounts still targeting the Google-suggested metro area rather than the real 15–30 mile service radius).
- **New service-line campaign builds** (e.g., adding a "heat pump install" campaign as that market shifts).

### Automatable vs. Not

| Genuinely automatable today | Still requires human judgment |
|---|---|
| Negative keyword mining from search-term reports | Deciding which leads are "good" vs. "junk" (context: is this a real emergency or a tire-kicker?) |
| Budget pacing alerts and auto-pause/resume rules | Creative concepting and offer design (what promotion will actually move this specific market) |
| Bid strategy (Smart Bidding / tROAS / tCPA already largely automated by Google/Meta) | Reading call recordings for tone, close-rate coaching, and quality judgment |
| Offline conversion upload (once integrated) | Negotiating/interpreting seasonality against a specific business's capacity (e.g., a 2-truck HVAC company can't absorb a lead surge) |
| Geo-radius enforcement / exclusion rules | Relationship and trust-building with the business owner (the "who do I call when something's wrong" function) |
| Basic A/B ad copy rotation | Complex, multi-variable campaign strategy tied to business goals (e.g., "we want higher-margin installs, not maintenance calls") |
| Call transcription and basic sentiment/keyword tagging (booked vs. not, spam signals) | Final judgment call on lead quality scoring that ties back to actual revenue/won jobs |

**Read for product design:** The mechanical, repetitive layers (pacing, negatives, bid rules, offline conversion sync, geo enforcement) are exactly the layer software already automates well (Google/Meta Smart Bidding did much of this already). The defensible, hard-to-automate layer is **judgment about lead quality** — and that's precisely the layer that requires closing the attribution loop back to the CRM/job outcome, not just the ad platform's own signals.

## 3. The Attribution Problem

Local service leads arrive through **phone call, form fill, text/SMS, and walk-in** — and the paid channel (Google, Meta) only natively "sees" the click, not what happened after. Closing this loop is the single biggest technical/product opportunity identified in this research.

### How good operators close the loop
1. **Call tracking** (CallRail is the category leader): dynamic number insertion captures the Google Click ID (gclid) on the visitor session, so when a call comes in, CallRail can match it back to the exact campaign/ad group/keyword and push it to Google Ads as an **offline conversion**.
2. **CRM/job-outcome import**: once a call or form lead becomes an actual booked/completed job with a dollar value, that revenue is uploaded back into the ad platform (Google Ads "Store Sales"/offline conversion import, using gclid matching) so the algorithm optimizes toward **revenue**, not just clicks.
3. **Native CRM integrations**: ServiceTitan has a direct Google Ads integration — it auto-creates a matching campaign structure, tags calls classified as "Lead," and has a "Revenue Import" feature that sends completed-job dollar values back into Google Ads tied to the gclid, creating a "ServiceTitan Completed Jobs Conversion Action."
4. **Zapier/middleware glue**: for CRMs without native integrations (Housecall Pro, Jobber), Zapier-based flows push "new appointment" or "job completed" events into Google Ads as offline conversions — but this is brittle, delayed, and often misconfigured by SMBs without technical help.

### The reputation/messaging layer (adjacent but relevant to attribution)
Podium ($399–$599/mo core tiers, effectively $450–$700+/mo with add-ons) and Broadly (~$799/mo bundles) already sell HVAC/plumbing/auto-repair shops on **review management + texting + basic call tracking**, which is adjacent to — but not the same as — ad-spend attribution. This shows SMBs in these verticals **already pay $400–$800/month for point software that touches customer communication**, which is a useful pricing anchor.

**Read for product design:** The defensible wedge is **not** better creative or better bidding (Google/Meta already automate that) — it's **being the system of record that ties ad spend → call/form → CRM job outcome → revenue**, and feeding that back into the ad platforms as offline conversions in near-real-time. ServiceTitan already does this for its own large customers; the gap is the long tail of Housecall Pro/Jobber/HighLevel shops who don't have (or can't afford/configure) this loop. CallRail is the closest existing competitor/complement in this specific niche.

## 4. The "Lead Quality" Gap

Direct evidence that SMB owners judge marketing by whether **good** jobs come in, not by CTR/CPM/ROAS:

- One marketing-ops source frames it directly: *"An agency might show that your phone rang 80 times last month, but if 50 of those calls were from outside your service area, 20 were people looking for free advice, and 5 were existing clients with billing questions, you effectively received 5 leads—not 80"* — illustrating that **raw call volume is treated as a "nearly useless metric"** by sophisticated operators, and what matters is qualified, bookable leads. ([mosierdata.com](https://mosierdata.com/2025/05/14/why-isnt-the-phone-ringing/))
- Legal marketing commentary: *"A ringing phone isn't always a sign your marketing is working—if you're fielding calls that don't convert into cases, the problem is lead quality"* ([luckyfishmedia.com](https://www.luckyfishmedia.com/2026/why-your-law-firm-isnt-getting-good-leads-even-if-the-phone-is-ringing/))
- The most common owner frustration is described as: *"the phone doesn't ring often enough, website inquiries remain inconsistent, and growth feels slower than it should"* — i.e., owners describe the problem in terms of **phone activity and business outcomes**, never in ad-platform metrics. ([miagency.ca](https://www.miagency.ca/why-your-small-business-isnt-getting-enough-leads-and-how-to-fix-it/))
- Evidence from the lead-gen marketplaces (Angi/Thumbtack/HomeAdvisor) reinforces the same pattern from the buy-side: contractors describe **shared leads closing at only 10–20%**, with **"burned budgets and disconnected phone numbers masquerading as qualified leads"** and a **documented 75% "ghost rate"** on some platforms — the complaint is never about CPC or impression share, it's entirely about whether the lead was real and turned into a job. ([homeshowoff.com](https://homeshowoff.com/real-cost-of-contractor-leads/), [allbetterapp.com](https://allbetterapp.com/allbetter-vs-homeadvisor-vs-thumbtack-vs-angi/))

**What a product needs to measure to speak their language:**
1. **Booked jobs and $ revenue per ad dollar** — not clicks, not CTR, not "leads."
2. **Job/lead quality classification** — in-area vs. out-of-area, real emergency vs. tire-kicker, spam/robocall vs. genuine — ideally auto-tagged from call transcripts and CRM disposition codes.
3. **Cost per booked job (not cost per lead)** — the gap between LSA's reported "cost per lead" (~$39–$59) and the real "cost per booked job" (accounting for the ~43–45% book rate) is the number owners actually care about.
4. **Plain-language framing**: "You spent $2,400 this month and got 14 real jobs worth $18,200" beats any ROAS/CPM dashboard. This is the core insight for a product's UI/reporting layer — it should speak in **jobs and dollars**, never platform jargon.

## 5. Pricing Models That Work on SMBs

| Model | How it's used today | What SMBs tolerate |
|---|---|---|
| **Percentage of ad spend** | Industry-standard for PPC agencies, 10–20% of monthly spend, decreasing as budget scales (e.g., $10K spend → $1K–$2K management fee) | Dominant at larger spend levels ($10K+/mo); creates a perceived conflict of interest (agency incentivized to spend more, not perform better) — a documented SMB complaint |
| **Flat monthly fee** | More common under $10K/mo spend; $500–$750/mo typical for small accounts, $1,500–$2,500/mo for mid-sized | Preferred by budget-conscious SMBs because it's predictable; retainers range $1,500 (basic) to $10,000+ (comprehensive) |
| **Per-lead / pay-per-lead** | Angi Leads (~$288–300/yr fee + $15–85/lead), Thumbtack ($10–$80+/lead), HomeAdvisor | Widely used but **widely hated** — shared leads (3–8 contractors per lead) close at only 10–20%, effective cost per booked job often $150–$400+; this model has the worst reputation among contractors of any pricing approach found in this research |
| **Hybrid** | 73% of agencies reportedly use hybrid pricing by 2026 (flat base + performance/spend component) | Emerging as the most balanced/accepted approach — aligns incentives while giving SMBs budget predictability |
| **Point-SaaS flat subscription** (comparable/adjacent category) | Podium ($399–$599/mo core, up to $700+/mo with add-ons), Broadly (~$799/mo bundles), HighLevel ($97/mo entry) | Demonstrates local service businesses **already pay $100–$800/month** for adjacent software (reviews, texting, basic CRM) — useful anchor for ACV expectations of a new tool |

**Realistic ACV and churn for SMB SaaS in this space:**
- SMB SaaS with ACV under ~$10K/year tolerates **3–5% monthly churn** (i.e., ~15–25% *annual* churn is typical and considered healthy, not alarming), versus enterprise SaaS needing <1% monthly churn.
- **Critical pattern: churn concentrates in the first 90 days** — one source cites 43% of all SMB losses happen in the first quarter post-purchase, and up to 70% of churn overall happens in the first 90 days. Strong onboarding (time-to-first-value under 7 days) is associated with ~50% lower churn.
- **Annual contracts suppress churn by 40–60%** versus month-to-month billing — a strong lever if the product wants to reduce churn risk, though SMBs in this space are also known to resist long lock-ins given historical bad experiences with agencies withholding account access (see Section 6).
- Given the point-SaaS comparables above ($97–$800/mo), a realistic ACV for a mid-market attribution/optimization SaaS in this space is likely **$1,200–$6,000/year** ($100–$500/mo), positioned as either a flat fee or a modest hybrid (small flat base + smaller % of spend, e.g., 5–8%, well under the 10–20% pure-agency rate, since the product isn't doing full-service management).

## 6. Trust and Onboarding

**What makes an owner hand over ad account access:**
- The single biggest trust issue identified is **historical bad behavior by agencies**: agencies creating and controlling ad accounts, then refusing to hand them back when the relationship ends, causing SMBs to lose years of campaign history, audience data, and conversion tracking if they switch providers.
- The emerging best practice/expectation is **"Partner Access"** (Google's and Meta's delegated-access models) rather than agencies creating accounts under their own ownership — this lets the business retain the underlying asset (account, pixel, conversion history) while granting revocable access.
- A cited rule of thumb: *"If your agency won't give ad account access, you should be asking why not and see that as a red flag"* — meaning **sophisticated SMB buyers now actively screen for this**, and a product/vendor that defaults to client-owned accounts + revocable delegated access has a trust advantage baked in from the start.

**Common objections (synthesized from the trust research + broader SMB software adoption patterns):**
1. "I don't understand what you're doing with my money" (opacity of ad platforms).
2. "If I stop paying you, do I lose everything?" (data/account portability fear).
3. "How do I know the leads are real?" (ties directly to Section 4 — the core value prop).
4. "I already pay for [ServiceTitan/Podium/HighLevel] — why do I need another tool?" (point-solution fatigue).
5. Prior bad experience with a lead-gen marketplace (Angi/Thumbtack) souring trust in *any* "we'll get you leads" pitch.

**How incumbents overcome them:**
- Google/Meta's own delegated **Partner Access** / Business Manager permission systems (client retains ownership, agency/tool gets scoped, revocable access) is now considered the safe default and something buyers actively look for.
- Transparent, plain-language reporting tied to jobs/revenue (not platform jargon) builds trust faster than technical dashboards (ties to Section 4).
- Point solutions like Podium/Broadly overcome onboarding friction with **white-glove setup and named account reps** — a signal that this market still expects human-assisted onboarding even for SaaS, not pure self-serve, at least initially.

**Sources:** [Why Agencies Refuse to Turn Over Ad Accounts](https://innovisionbiz.com/why-agencies-refuse-to-turn-over-google-ads-accounts/), [Why Businesses Must Own Their Ad Accounts](https://www.thebrandamp.com/blog/why-businesses-must-own-their-ad-accounts-first-party-data-switching-agencies/), [How to Grant Google Ads Account Access 2026](https://www.leadsie.com/blog/how-to-grant-or-request-access-to-google-ad-accounts).

## 7. Seasonality and Spend Floor

**Below what monthly spend do paid ads stop working?**
- **Google Ads**: Multiple sources converge on **$1,000–$2,500/month as the practical floor** for local service businesses — below roughly $200–$300/month, there isn't enough click/conversion volume for the platform's bidding algorithms to gather meaningful data or optimize, and campaigns effectively can't "learn." $1,000–$2,500/mo typically yields 15–30 conversions/month, which is closer to the minimum needed for algorithmic learning phases to exit cleanly.
- **Meta/Facebook Ads**: Similar dynamic — setting daily budgets too low burns spend on impressions without enough conversions to exit the learning phase; practical minimums are usually framed in daily terms (~$20–$50/day, i.e., ~$600–$1,500/month) for the algorithm to function.
- **By vertical**, the effective floor scales with CPL: a vertical with $150+ CPLs (roofing, PI law) needs a much higher minimum monthly budget just to generate a statistically useful handful of leads (e.g., $1,500/mo at $150 CPL = only 10 leads) than a vertical with $20–$40 CPLs (HVAC, electrical, gyms), where the same $1,500 buys 40–75 leads.
- **Seasonality compounds this**: HVAC/roofing/moving/landscaping are highly seasonal, and operators are advised to flex budget 50–100% up during peak windows (heat waves, storm season, summer moving season) and pause or redirect during shoulder seasons — meaning the realistic "floor" for these verticals isn't a flat number but a seasonally-varying minimum that a rigid low-budget account can't accommodate at all in peak season.

**Implication for addressable market:** This defines the bottom of the market at roughly **$1,000–$1,500/month in ad spend** as the point below which a business (a) can't gather enough data for platforms to optimize and (b) generates too few leads/month for any attribution or optimization layer to add measurable value. Below that floor, a business is better served by organic/referral-focused tools (reviews, SEO, GBP optimization) than paid-media optimization software — which has direct implications for the bottom of a SaaS product's ICP (target businesses spending at least ~$1,500–$2,000/mo on paid ads).

**Sources:** [Google Ads Minimum Budget Guide 2026](https://www.get-ryze.ai/blog/google-ads-minimum-budget-guide-2026), [Meta Ads Minimum Daily Budget 2026](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/meta-ads-minimum-daily-budget-2026), [How Much Should My Digital Advertising Budget Be](https://web.uri.edu/risbdc/how-much-should-my-digital-advertising-budget-be/), [HVAC Budget Pacing/Seasonality](https://hustlemarketers.com/hvac-ppc-marketing/).

## Key Data Gaps / Where to Extrapolate Cautiously

1. **Salon/barber and auto-repair CRM ecosystems** were not deeply researched (time-boxed) — worth a follow-up pass if those verticals become priority.
2. **Moving company and auto-repair CRM API openness** (SmartMoving, Tekmetric, Shop-Ware, Mitchell1) was not verified — flagged as thin.
3. **A directly-reported "attribution SaaS for SMB" pricing/churn benchmark does not appear to exist publicly** — the ACV/pricing recommendation in Section 5 is synthesized from adjacent point-solution pricing (Podium, Broadly, HighLevel) and general SMB SaaS churn data, not a category-specific study.
4. **Nextdoor's realistic lead-quality data is a single case study** (Visiting Angels going from $73 to $26 CPL) — not representative of typical performance and shouldn't be treated as a category benchmark.
5. **ServiceTitan's own API/marketplace access tier and pricing for its Ads Optimizer/Marketing Pro product** was not fully priced out — worth deeper research before building a competing/complementary integration, since ServiceTitan is both a potential integration partner and, via Marketing Pro, a potential competitor for the largest accounts in HVAC/plumbing.
