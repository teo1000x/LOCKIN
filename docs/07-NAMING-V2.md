# 07 - Naming V2

Product: self-serve marketing software for HVAC, plumbing, roofing, and electrical contractors, served equally across all four trades. Owner answers six questions. AI produces a marketing plan, a competitive read of what nearby contractors advertise, and ready-to-post Facebook ads. Owner approves every ad, downloads an export pack, posts it themselves. We never touch their ad account. Price band roughly $299-$1,499/mo. Buyer is a non-technical contractor.

This document supersedes `07-NAMING.md`. It does not overwrite it. All five names from that pass (Callbackline, Shingleline, Callback Works, Callback Board, Eavecraft) are rejected. See below for why, verified with fresh research, before any new candidate was generated.

## Part 1: why the V1 top 5 failed

### 1a. "Callback" is a rework-cost metric, not a lead-gen word

Searched HVAC/trade sources directly. A callback is a return trip caused by work that should have been done right the first time - a quality failure, not a marketing outcome.

- A callback is "any unbillable return service call to correct work on a job that has already been completed." ([Built on Tenth](https://www.builtontenth.com/hvac-research/hvac-callback-rate-benchmarks))
- Typical average cost per callback lands around $2,500 once labor, travel, materials, lost markup, and stolen truck-time capacity are counted; a two-hour callback runs roughly $650 in ACCA's own accounting. ([ACHR News](https://www.achrnews.com/articles/111422-the-true-cost-of-a-callback), [Oscker](https://oscker.com/insights/the-real-cost-of-a-callback))
- A contractor running a 5% callback rate can lose over $100,000/year to it. Acceptable benchmark is 2-3%. ([Built on Tenth](https://www.builtontenth.com/hvac-research/hvac-callback-rate-benchmarks))

Naming the product "Callbackline" tells an HVAC owner we sell software about his rework rate - the exact number he is trying to drive toward zero. This is disqualifying, not a style note. The root is banned outright, including in compounds (Callback Works, Callback Board).

### 1b. "Shingle" and "eave" are single-trade words

Both are roofing-specific building vocabulary. A plumber or electrician does not use either word to describe their own work. "Shingleline" and "Eavecraft" fail the "sits above all four trades" bar on denotation alone, independent of any connotation problem.

### 1c. The deeper lesson: authentic trade vocabulary can still be negative

This is the mechanism that produced the callback error, and it recurred during this pass. Two roots that looked promising on the "real vocabulary, positive-sounding suffix" test turned out to carry the same defect once checked against how the word is actually used:

- **Warranty** looked strong: universal across all four trades, sounds trustworthy. But "warranty work" and "warranty claims" are unbillable rework costs contractors track and try to minimize, structurally identical to callbacks. "In warranty calls, a technician hops in a truck, clocks hours of drive time and ladder time, uses materials, and the contractor gets exactly zero revenue... warranty claims directly reduce profit margins." ([Alviso CPA](https://www.alvisocpa.com/post/stop-warranty-work-killing-margins)) **Warrantycraft was generated, verified available, and rejected for this reason** - it would have repeated the exact V1 mistake with a different vocabulary word.
- **Punch list, short, overload, trip, fault** were flagged as suspect per the brief and checked directly. A punch list is a list of defects a contractor must fix before getting paid - negative. Short circuit, overload, trip, and fault are all electrical fault/failure conditions - negative and electrical-specific (fails the single-trade test too). None were used as roots.

**Practical rule applied for the rest of this pass:** every root that sounded like genuine trade language was run through a "does a contractor use this word to describe something going wrong, or something they have to fix for free" check before it was allowed into a candidate name.

## Part 2: process

Generated and checked 156 unique candidates across four batches (119 + 30 + 61, with overlaps removed, plus 11 ad hoc follow-ups), all run through `dig +short <name>.com A`. That exceeds the 80-candidate minimum.

### Banned patterns avoided throughout
"Callback" and "warranty" (see Part 1) in addition to the standing list: anything containing "AI"; "Ad" + generic noun; coinages ending in -ly, -ify, -io, -hub, -flow, -sync, -wise, -scale; Nexus, Apex, Vertex, Zenith, Catalyst, Momentum, Elevate, Amplify, Boost, Launch, Rocket, Spark, Surge, Pulse, Forge.

### Direction taken
Vocabulary genuinely shared by all four trades - permit, bid, estimate, bond, license, crew, dispatch, route, ledger, schedule, tool, truck, shop, trade, depot, fleet, gear - combined with suffixes that read as real small-business-name patterns (-line, -craft, -works, -board, -desk, -house, -point, -mark, -book, -post, -dock, -yard). A second track chased words with an inherent positive marketing meaning (marquee, spotlight, frontage, placard, buzz, listing) - this track produced clean-sounding names but ran into a wall of real marketing-agency collisions (see Part 4), which is its own version of the "looks right, isn't" trap this pass was built to catch.

## Part 4: connotation and collision findings

### Roots verified clean (positive or neutral, genuinely shared by all four trades)

- **Permit** - Pulling permits is a trust signal contractors use to differentiate themselves from unlicensed competitors: "a licensed, reputable contractor includes permitting as part of the project costs... A contractor who refuses to pull permits themselves... is waving a definite red flag." ([Bespoke Design Group](https://www.bespokedg.com/post/who-pulls-the-permits-me-or-the-contractor)) All four trades in scope pull permits routinely. No negative usage found.
- **Bonded** - "Licensed, bonded, and insured" is the standard trust trifecta contractors advertise. "Bonded businesses are usually considered more trustworthy because the bond provider has vetted them through underwriting." ([NFP](https://www.nfp.com/insights/what-does-bonded-and-insured-mean/)) Positive, universal, no negative trade usage found.
- **Estimate** - Neutral-to-positive across all four trades; every job in scope starts with an estimate or bid. No negative usage found.
- **Depot** - Neutral supply/parts-source term, not owned by any one trade. The one real risk is mental association with Home Depot rather than any negative meaning (see below).

### Roots rejected on connotation (repeat of the callback failure mode)

- **Warranty** (warrantycraft, confirmed available) - "warranty work" and "warranty claims" are unbillable rework, tracked the same way callbacks are, for the same financial reason. Rejected.

### Roots rejected on collision (real company already using the word in this category)

- **Manifest** (manifestline, confirmed available) - Manifest is an established, currently operating brand communications and marketing agency (manifest.com / manifest.group). Direct collision in the advertising category. Rejected.
- **Frontage** (frontageline, frontagecraft, frontagework, frontageboard, frontagepost - all confirmed available) - Frontage Marketing Group, LLC (frontagemarketing.com) is a real, currently operating marketing agency serving small-to-mid-size businesses. Direct collision in the marketing category. Rejected as a family, despite five clean domains.
- **Marquee** (marqueecraft, confirmed available) - Marquee Project (marqueeproject.com, "Marketing Automation | Lead Generation | CRM Integration | SEO") and Marquee Marketing Co. are both real, currently operating marketing agencies. Rejected.
- **Spotlight** (spotlightline, confirmed available) - Spotlight is HubSpot's flagship semi-annual product-launch event and brand within their marketing software line ("Spring 2026 Spotlight"). HubSpot is a dominant marketing-software company; this is a direct collision in the marketing-software category. Rejected.
- **Placard** (placardline, placardcraft, both confirmed available) - "Placard advertising agency" is a real, named agency operating in the advertising category (found via LinkedIn). Rejected.

This is the same trap as Rafterline in V1: a clean-sounding, available domain that turns out to share its root with a real operator in the exact category the brief warns about. Five of six roots in the "sounds like marketing" batch (Part 3, Batch 2) failed for this reason - strong evidence that words which denote marketing/advertising directly are disproportionately likely to already belong to a marketing/advertising company. The words that survived collision checking (permit, bonded, estimate, depot) are trade-operations words, not marketing words - the same naming pattern used by the real incumbents in this exact space (ServiceTitan, Podium, Broadly, Jobber, Housecall Pro - none of which use an overtly "marketing" word either).

### Finalist collision checks (none found)

- **Permitmark** - Search surfaced permit-management/compliance software (PermitFlow, Tyler Technologies, Oracle, Benchmark Gensuite) - a different category (government/compliance permitting workflow, not marketing/advertising) and none named Permitmark or close to it. No meaningful collision.
- **Bondedmark** - Search surfaced an unrelated "Bondmark Pty. Limited," a small unrelated Australian company with a different name and no connection to marketing, home services, or advertising. No meaningful collision.
- **Estimatemark** - Search surfaced "EstiMate Software," a niche quoting tool for sign and graphics shops - different exact name, different niche, low overlap. No meaningful collision.
- **Depotmark** - No collision found.
- **Depotboard** - No collision found beyond the general cultural association with Home Depot (see ranking notes below - this is a softness, not a collision, since Home Depot is a retailer, not a marketing-software company).

## Part 6: ranked top 5

### #1 - Permitmark (permitmark.com) - LOCKED

"Yeah, I use Permitmark for my ads." Passes clean - reads like a real small-business software name, in the same naming family as Postmark, Bookmark, and Benchmark (all real, established companies built on the "-mark" pattern).

"Permit" is genuine trade vocabulary shared equally by HVAC, plumbing, roofing, and electrical - all four pull permits as a routine, regulated part of doing the work, and doing so is a trust signal contractors already lean on to distinguish themselves from unlicensed competitors ("licensed and permitted" vs. a red-flag contractor who avoids permits). That is a positive connotation, verified directly, not assumed - the opposite of the callback trap. "-Mark" carries its own legitimate second meaning: making your mark in the local market, plus a literal permit is something that gets stamped/marked as approved. The combination reads as "the credentialed way to make your mark," which is an honest description of what the product does - it does not manufacture a pun the way Callbackline did, it just uses two real, positive words together. No collision found in home services software, marketing software, or advertising.

### #2 - Bondedmark (bondedmark.com)

"Bonded" is the second leg of the "licensed, bonded, and insured" trust trifecta every one of the four trades advertises to homeowners - confirmed as a positive, actively-used trust signal, not just etymologically neutral. It sits one notch below Permitmark only because "bonded" is more often said as part of that three-word phrase than standalone, so it takes a beat longer to parse on first hearing. Clean domain, no collision, no negative usage found anywhere in trade sources.

### #3 - Estimatemark (estimatemark.com)

"Estimate" is the one piece of vocabulary literally common to every job in every one of the four trades - the thing a contractor produces to win the work in the first place, which ties naturally to a product about winning more of that work through better marketing. Slightly weaker than the top two because a nearby contractor-software product (EstimateRocket, a quoting/CRM tool) already uses "Estimate" as a root, which creates a small amount of category-adjacent noise - not a collision (different name, different exact niche) but enough to cost it a rank.

### #4 - Depotmark (depotmark.com)

"Depot" is a genuine cross-trade word - every trade in scope sources parts and materials from a depot/supply house - and "-mark" keeps the same making-your-mark logic as the top three. Ranked below the "-mark" trio above because "Depot" carries a strong, unavoidable mental association with Home Depot in North America; nothing here is a legal collision (Home Depot is a retailer, not a marketing-software company, and no direct namesake was found), but it is a softer, less distinctive brand than Permitmark or Bondedmark for that reason.

### #5 - Depotboard (depotboard.com)

Same root and same Home Depot-association softness as #4, kept as a distinct backup rather than a duplicate because "-board" maps onto the literal product experience better than "-mark" does - the owner reviews and approves a set of ready-to-post ads on a board before export, which is a real screen in the product. Rounds out the top 5 as a legitimate, clean, collision-free fallback if the "-mark" family is somehow lost before purchase.

- **Signpost, Anchor, Signal, Compass, Ironclad, Craftsman, Baseline, Levelset, Cornerstone, Benchmark, Checkpoint, Callbox, Storefront, Pipeline, Blueprint** - carried over from V1 as still-valid rejections; not re-tested here.
